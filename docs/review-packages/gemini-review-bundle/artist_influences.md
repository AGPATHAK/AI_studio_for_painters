# Artist Influences

## Purpose

This document captures the artistic influences that shaped the critique philosophy and visual reasoning system.

These influences are used:
- pedagogically,
- structurally,
- and conceptually.

The goal is:
- principle extraction,
not:
- stylistic imitation.

---

## Edward Wesson

### What Is Valued

Edward Wesson strongly influenced the project’s emphasis on:
- atmospheric simplification,
- shape economy,
- connected washes,
- selective detail,
- and painterly editing.

---

### Major Lessons

#### Large Shape Dominance

Complex scenes should often be reduced into:
- a few dominant masses,
- connected shadow structures,
- and broad tonal groupings.

This directly shaped:
- anti-fragmentation doctrine,
- value grouping heuristics,
- and simplification workflows.

---

#### Edge Economy

Wesson uses:
- selective sharpness,
- soft transitions,
- and implied structure.

Recurring lesson:
> Not everything deserves description.

This heavily influenced:
- lost-and-found edge philosophy,
- edge economy,
- and focal hierarchy critique.

---

#### Atmospheric Editing

Wesson often suppresses:
- unnecessary detail,
- architectural clutter,
- repetitive marks,
- and secondary contrast.

This inspired recurring critique guidance:
- quiet the background,
- merge secondary forms,
- reduce edge count,
- simplify before refining.

---

### Palette Tendencies

Typical Wesson-like characteristics:
- muted earths,
- restrained blues,
- atmospheric greys,
- warm neutrals,
- limited saturation.

High chroma is used sparingly.

---

## Edward Seago

### What Is Valued

Edward Seago influenced the project’s emphasis on:
- atmosphere,
- tonal subtlety,
- restrained contrast,
- emotional quietness,
- and spatial depth.

---

### Major Lessons

#### High-Key Structure

Seago often works with:
- compressed value ranges,
- subtle tonal transitions,
- and restrained contrast relationships.

This shaped:
- high-key watercolor studies,
- soft atmospheric handling,
- and tonal cohesion doctrine.

---

#### Atmosphere Through Omission

Seago frequently avoids:
- excessive detail,
- aggressive contrast,
- and chromatic noise.

Recurring lesson:
> Atmosphere often emerges from restraint.

---

#### Tonal Cohesion

Seago’s paintings feel unified because:
- values remain compressed,
- chroma stays controlled,
- and transitions remain coherent.

This strongly influenced:
- tonal unity critique,
- chroma hierarchy,
- and atmospheric workflows.

---

## Line-and-Wash Traditions

The project also draws from line-and-wash traditions emphasizing:
- selective drawing,
- rhythm of marks,
- structural economy,
- and wash dominance.

Key principle:
- lines should support washes,
not imprison them.

---

## Restrained Palette Doctrine

Repeatedly preferred characteristics:
- limited pigments,
- neutralized mixtures,
- warm/cool orchestration,
- atmospheric greys.

Common pigments discussed:
- ultramarine,
- cobalt,
- burnt sienna,
- raw sienna,
- Payne’s grey.

Reasoning:
- limited palettes improve harmony,
- reduce chromatic chaos,
- and simplify decision-making.

---

## Atmospheric Simplification

A central influence across multiple artists.

Core doctrine:
> Simplification creates atmosphere.

Methods include:
- reducing edge count,
- compressing detail,
- muting secondary chroma,
- merging distant forms,
- simplifying backgrounds.

---

## Relationship to Critique Philosophy

These influences are source lineages, not style targets. When influence language appears in workflow prompts, it should point to transferable principles such as atmospheric simplification, value grouping, edge economy, or chroma hierarchy rather than asking for imitation.

---

## Influence on Critique Philosophy

These artistic influences collectively shaped the system toward:
- painterliness over realism,
- atmosphere over description,
- structure over detail,
- and abstraction over literal transcription.

Recurring guiding question:
> What can be removed without weakening the painting?
