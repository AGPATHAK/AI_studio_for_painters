# Repaintability Framework

## Purpose

Repaintability is the practical clarity that allows a painter to move from critique into a stronger next attempt.

This framework defines what makes critique actionable without reducing painting to instructions. Its purpose is to keep AI-assisted critique anchored in learning, judgment, and manual repainting.

---

## Core Definition

A critique is repaintable when it helps the painter understand:
- what is visually weakening the painting,
- why it matters,
- what should change first,
- what should be preserved,
- and how to approach the next pass or repaint.

Repaintability is not the same as ease. A recommendation may be difficult, but it should be clear enough to guide the painter's next decision.

---

## Repaintability Requirements

### 1. Structural Specificity

The critique should name the visual structure involved.

Useful:
- "The shadow masses are fragmented across the middle distance."
- "The focal contrast is being diluted by hard edges in the corners."

Weak:
- "Improve the composition."
- "Make it more painterly."

---

### 2. Priority

The critique should distinguish first-order problems from refinements.

Use:
- first: the change most likely to improve the painting's read,
- second: a supporting adjustment,
- optional: a refinement that should not distract from the main repair.

Avoid treating every possible improvement as equally urgent.

---

### 3. Preserved Strengths

Repaint guidance should identify what must not be lost.

Examples:
- preserve the freshest wash,
- keep the main light shape,
- protect the existing atmospheric softness,
- retain the successful drawing rhythm.

This prevents critique from becoming destructive correction.

---

### 4. Sequence

The painter should know when to act.

Typical repaint sequence:
1. simplify the value plan,
2. establish dominant masses,
3. protect focal hierarchy,
4. subordinate secondary passages,
5. add final accents sparingly.

For watercolor, sequence matters especially because freshness and paper integrity are fragile.

---

### 5. Transferable Principle

Each critique should leave behind a lesson that applies beyond the current image.

Examples:
- merge first, separate later only if necessary,
- reserve hard edges for important transitions,
- mute secondary chroma before adding focal accents,
- repaint fresh when the surface has become labored.

---

## Repaintability Levels

### High Repaintability

The painter can clearly identify:
- the visual problem,
- the affected region or passage,
- the first repaint action,
- what to preserve,
- and how success should look.

Example:
> Rebuild the middle-distance shadow as one connected value family before adding any branch or roof accents.

---

### Medium Repaintability

The critique identifies the problem and principle, but the exact repaint sequence needs interpretation.

Example:
> The background has too many competing hard edges. Reduce edge activity outside the focal area.

---

### Low Repaintability

The critique names a general weakness but does not guide the next attempt clearly.

Example:
> The painting needs stronger atmosphere.

Low-repaintability critique should be revised before it becomes guidance or an edit request.

---

## From Critique to Repaint

### Diagnostic Critique

Names what is happening visually.

Repaintability contribution:
- identifies the root issue.

---

### Teaching Critique

Explains why the issue matters.

Repaintability contribution:
- gives the painter a principle to carry into the next attempt.

---

### Prescriptive Critique

Names what should be changed.

Repaintability contribution:
- creates an actionable repaint plan.

---

### Demonstrative Critique

Shows or describes a possible correction.

Repaintability contribution:
- clarifies the intended direction through comparison.

Demonstration should remain subordinate to repaint learning.

---

## Repaint Success Signals

A repaint has likely improved when:
- the image reads more clearly from a distance,
- dominant masses are stronger,
- focal hierarchy is clearer,
- secondary passages compete less,
- edge economy feels more intentional,
- chroma supports depth and focus,
- watercolor passages remain fresh,
- and the painter can explain the change in visual terms.

Success is not measured by polish alone.
