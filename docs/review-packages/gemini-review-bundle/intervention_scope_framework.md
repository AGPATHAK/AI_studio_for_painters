# Intervention Scope Framework

## Purpose

This document defines how broad a correction or demonstration may be.

The goal is to protect selective intervention doctrine: change only what teaches the painter something clear, and preserve the passages that already work.

---

## Core Doctrine

Intervention scope should be no larger than the visual problem requires.

A useful intervention is:
- limited,
- interpretable,
- traceable to a critique,
- respectful of successful passages,
- and safe for repaint learning.

Avoid:
- global beautification,
- unrelated corrections,
- style replacement,
- over-rendering,
- and corrections that hide the original painting problem.

---

## Scope Classes

### Local

Affects a small passage or specific visual incident.

Use for:
- one hard edge,
- a small high-chroma distraction,
- a tangent,
- a local dark accent,
- a single overdescribed detail cluster.

Allowed behavior:
- soften,
- mute,
- remove,
- merge,
- clarify.

---

### Regional

Affects a connected passage or visual family.

Use for:
- a background area,
- a shadow family,
- a flower cluster,
- a roofline or tree mass,
- a group of secondary forms.

Allowed behavior:
- unify,
- compress,
- group,
- quiet,
- reduce edge activity.

---

### Compositional

Affects the painting's overall read or attention path while still preserving the subject.

Use for:
- focal hierarchy,
- corner noise,
- dominant value mass,
- broad atmospheric simplification,
- major directional flow.

Allowed behavior:
- redistribute emphasis,
- subordinate competing passages,
- clarify dominant movement,
- protect the focal area.

Compositional interventions should be used cautiously because they can easily become redesign.

---

### Global Study

Creates a study or comparison artifact rather than a finished correction.

Use for:
- value-only study,
- Notan,
- tonal mask,
- simplified repaint plan,
- full-image atmospheric comparison.

Allowed behavior:
- simplify the whole image for learning,
- separate value from color,
- test alternate focal hierarchy,
- clarify repaint strategy.

Global study is acceptable when it is explicitly educational. It should not be presented as the corrected painting.

---

## Prohibited Behaviors

Do not use an intervention to:
- improve everything,
- add decorative detail,
- finish unresolved areas,
- replace the painter's drawing,
- imitate a named artist's surface style,
- introduce new subject matter,
- remove ambiguity that is already working,
- or make watercolor passages look digitally polished.

---

## Escalation Rules

### Local to Regional

Escalate when the same problem repeats across a connected passage.

Example:
- one hard edge is local,
- equal hard edges across a background tree mass are regional.

---

### Regional to Compositional

Escalate when a passage affects the whole eye path or focal hierarchy.

Example:
- a loud corner cluster may require compositional quieting if it pulls attention away from the focal area.

---

### Compositional to Global Study

Escalate only when the problem cannot be clarified by a selective correction.

Example:
- widespread value fragmentation may be better handled through a value-only study than a broad corrective edit.

---

## Interpretability Doctrine

After an intervention, the painter should be able to say:
- what changed,
- why it changed,
- what stayed untouched,
- and what lesson should carry into the repaint.

If the painter cannot identify the lesson, the intervention was too broad, too decorative, or insufficiently tied to critique.

---

## Watercolor Scope Sensitivity

Watercolor corrections carry special risk.

Prefer repaint guidance over correction when:
- paper fatigue is central,
- the passage is already overworked,
- freshness is the main issue,
- or the correction would require repeated patching.

Useful watercolor interventions should demonstrate:
- simpler wash structure,
- fewer interruptions,
- more intentional edge economy,
- preserved luminosity,
- or clearer repaint sequence.

---

## Relationship to Edit Requests

Every conceptual edit request should name:
- scope class,
- target passage,
- protected passages,
- reason for scope,
- and fallback if the scope becomes too broad.
