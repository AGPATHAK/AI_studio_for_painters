# Watercolor Specific Doctrine

## Purpose

This document captures the watercolor-specific principles repeatedly emphasized throughout the critique system.

The focus is:
- freshness,
- atmosphere,
- edge sensitivity,
- transparency,
- and painterly restraint.

---

## Core Philosophy

Watercolor is treated as:
- a medium of controlled surrender,
- simplification,
- transparency,
- and selective control.

The goal is not:
- total precision,
- excessive correction,
- or over-rendered realism.

---

## Freshness Doctrine

Freshness is considered:
- fragile,
- valuable,
- and easily destroyed.

Common threats:
- repeated correction,
- excessive lifting,
- scrubbing,
- patching muddy passages.

Recurring principle:
> Repainting fresh is often better than patching endlessly.

---

## White Preservation Doctrine

Watercolor light usually comes from:
- preserved paper,
not:
- added opacity later.

Preferred workflow:
- preserve important whites early,
- simplify around light shapes,
- avoid late-stage rescue attempts.

---

## Edge Economy Doctrine

Edges are treated as:
- primary compositional tools.

Preferred characteristics:
- soft atmospheric transitions,
- selective hard edges,
- lost-and-found contours,
- varied edge behavior.

Avoid:
- outlining mentality,
- equal sharpness everywhere,
- excessive contour definition.

---

## Wash Doctrine

Good washes typically appear:
- confident,
- economical,
- and unified.

Preferred handling:
- larger connected passages,
- fewer unnecessary interruptions,
- decisive brush movement.

Avoid:
- timid patchwork washes,
- repetitive touching,
- fragmented application.

---

## Glazing Doctrine

Glazing is preferred over:
- scrubbing,
- aggressive lifting,
- or muddy repainting.

Useful glazing goals:
- chroma hierarchy,
- atmospheric cooling,
- shadow unification,
- value deepening.

---

## Simplification Doctrine

Watercolor benefits strongly from:
- shape economy,
- selective omission,
- and atmospheric editing.

Preferred:
- suggestive passages,
- grouped forms,
- implied structure.

Avoid:
- rendering everything equally,
- over-described detail,
- repetitive marks.

---

## Atmospheric Simplification Doctrine

Atmosphere often improves through:
- softened edges,
- quieter backgrounds,
- reduced chroma,
- compressed detail,
- and restrained contrast.

Recurring principle:
> Atmosphere is usually weakened by over-description.

---

## Brushwork Doctrine

Brushwork should:
- support rhythm,
- preserve vitality,
- and reinforce shape structure.

Preferred marks:
- economical,
- varied,
- and purposeful.

Avoid:
- mechanical repetition,
- over-brushing,
- and excessive correction.

---

## Chroma Hierarchy Doctrine

Preferred watercolor color handling:
- restrained palettes,
- atmospheric greys,
- warm/cool orchestration,
- selective saturation.

High chroma should be:
- limited,
- intentional,
- and compositionally justified.

---

## Paper Integrity Doctrine

Paper fatigue is treated seriously.

Common causes:
- excessive lifting,
- repeated wetting,
- overworking,
- aggressive correction.

Once surface vitality is lost:
- repainting is often preferable.

---

## Repaint Workflow Doctrine

A repaint is not considered failure.

Repainting is often:
- the fastest route to improvement,
- especially after structural insight emerges.

Preferred workflow:
1. Diagnose
2. Simplify
3. Rebuild structure
4. Repaint fresh

---

## Relationship to Workflows

These constraints should shape the selective interventions in [multimodal_workflows.md](multimodal_workflows.md) and the watercolor primitives in [prompt_primitives.md](prompt_primitives.md). The point is not to make corrections look finished, but to help the painter choose when to preserve, glaze, simplify, or repaint fresh.

---

## Guiding Watercolor Question

At every stage ask:
> Is this passage becoming fresher or more labored?
