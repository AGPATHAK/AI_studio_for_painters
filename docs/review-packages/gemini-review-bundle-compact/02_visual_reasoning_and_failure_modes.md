## Source Boundary: Visual Heuristics

These heuristics support painterly judgment, critique consistency, and AI-assisted correction workflows.

### Value Grouping Heuristics

Read from distance first. A painting should remain readable at thumbnail size, from across the room, and under squinting.

Loss of readability usually indicates:
- fragmentation,
- weak mass grouping,
- or excessive detail competition.

Strong paintings usually contain:
- a few dominant connected shapes,
- connected shadow families,
- unified midtone structures,
- simplified light groupings.

Avoid:
- salt-and-pepper value noise,
- disconnected dark accents,
- excessive tonal interruptions.

Recurring doctrine:
> Merge first, separate later only if necessary.

Secondary values should be compressed in:
- background passages,
- secondary objects,
- corner regions,
- distant forms.

Reserve strongest contrast for focal hierarchy.

### Edge Economy Heuristics

Hard edges are expensive because they attract attention.

Use them selectively:
- near focal areas,
- major plane changes,
- critical structural accents.

Atmosphere often improves when:
- contours dissolve,
- adjacent values merge,
- forms partially disappear.

Good paintings contain:
- lost edges,
- soft edges,
- broken edges,
- selective sharp accents.

Uniform edge treatment creates stiffness.

### Composition Heuristics

Protect focal hierarchy. The eye should know where to enter, where to pause, and where not to linger.

Focal hierarchy is controlled through:
- value contrast,
- edge sharpness,
- chroma,
- detail density.

Corners should rarely compete with the focal area.

Reduce near edges:
- contrast,
- edge activity,
- texture,
- chroma.

Avoid tangent clutter by using clear overlap, clear separation, or deliberate merging.

### Chroma Hierarchy Heuristics

High saturation everywhere weakens atmosphere.

Reserve strongest chroma for:
- focal regions,
- emotional accents,
- deliberate compositional emphasis.

Backgrounds and supporting areas usually benefit from:
- muted mixtures,
- atmospheric greys,
- lower saturation.

Typical atmospheric pattern:
- warmer foregrounds,
- cooler distance,
- softer distant transitions.

### Simplification Heuristics

Paint masses before objects. Large shape relationships matter more than object identity, decorative marks, or local detail.

Recurring critique question:
> What can be removed without harming the painting?

### AI-Assisted Critique Heuristics

Avoid global improvement.

Preferred:
- local interventions,
- interpretable changes,
- controlled demonstrations.

AI corrections exist to teach, clarify, and guide repaint decisions. They are not the final artwork.

## Source Boundary: Common Failure Modes

### Fragmented Value Structure

Symptoms:
- noisy appearance,
- weak readability,
- scattered darks,
- no dominant mass.

Likely causes:
- copying photo detail literally,
- excessive small contrasts,
- isolated tonal decisions.

Corrections:
- merge shadow families,
- simplify midtones,
- remove isolated accents.

Repaint strategy:
- rebuild major value masses first before detail.

### Weak Focal Contrast

Symptoms:
- unclear emphasis,
- wandering eye,
- equal importance everywhere.

Corrections:
- deepen focal darks,
- reduce peripheral contrast,
- sharpen focal transitions selectively.

### Too Many Hard Edges / Edge Equality

Symptoms:
- stiffness,
- visual noise,
- loss of atmosphere,
- everything competes.

Corrections:
- soften secondary contours,
- merge adjacent values,
- reserve sharp edges,
- lose background contours.

### Tangent Clutter and Corner Noise

Symptoms:
- accidental tension,
- visual irritation,
- eye trapped near edges,
- weak containment.

Corrections:
- separate shapes clearly,
- overlap decisively,
- reduce corner detail,
- lower corner contrast.

### Excessive Chroma / Decorative Color

Symptoms:
- loudness,
- visual fatigue,
- weak atmosphere,
- shallow depth,
- weak unity.

Corrections:
- mute secondary passages,
- preserve strongest chroma near focal area,
- introduce atmospheric greys,
- tie color to depth, temperature, focal hierarchy, and atmosphere.

### Detail Addiction

Symptoms:
- fragmented field,
- weak atmosphere,
- loss of dominance.

Corrections:
- paint masses before objects,
- remove unnecessary marks,
- simplify secondary forms.

### Watercolor Overworking

Symptoms:
- muddy passages,
- dead surface,
- chalky handling,
- damaged paper.

Corrections:
- stop earlier,
- repaint fresh passages,
- preserve wash integrity.

Doctrine:
- freshness is usually more valuable than incremental correction.

### Flat Spatial Depth

Symptoms:
- weak separation of planes,
- compressed space,
- lack of atmosphere.

Corrections:
- soften distant forms,
- cool distant passages,
- reduce distant detail.

### Subject-Specific Failures

Floral handling:
- overdescribed petals,
- busy flowers,
- decorative clutter.

Landscape handling:
- broken shadow architecture,
- disconnected landscape structure,
- object-by-object shadow painting.

Architecture handling:
- excessive structural precision,
- rigidity,
- drafting appearance.

## Source Boundary: Critique Patterns

### Preferred Critique Structure

1. Honest diagnosis
2. Critique type
3. Priority problems
4. Root-cause explanation
5. Specific interventions
6. Repaint guidance
7. Underlying principle

### Critique Type Patterns

Diagnostic critique identifies what is happening visually:
- "The value structure fragments because..."
- "The focal hierarchy weakens where..."
- "The eye is being pulled away by..."

Teaching critique explains why the issue matters:
- "This matters because the painting loses its distance read."
- "The principle here is to merge first, separate later."
- "Hard edges are expensive; spend them near the focal area."

Prescriptive critique guides the next repaint decision:
- "On the repaint, group the shadow family before adding accents."
- "Preserve the central light shape and quiet the surrounding contrast."
- "Delay petal accents until the flower mass reads as one shape."

Demonstrative critique shows a controlled alternative when comparison will clarify the lesson:
- "A useful demonstration would compress these secondary midtones."
- "A value-only study would show whether the shadow pattern is strong enough."

### Strong Diagnostic Language

Preferred phrasing:
- "The painting weakens where..."
- "The eye gets trapped because..."
- "This area competes unnecessarily."
- "The value structure fragments here."
- "This edge economy collapses because..."
- "The chroma hierarchy is flattening depth."

Avoid:
- vague praise,
- emotional generalities,
- decorative commentary,
- empty encouragement.

### Repaintability Pattern

Useful guidance should name:
- the first action,
- what to preserve,
- what to simplify,
- what to delay,
- and the success signal.

Example:
> Rebuild the background as two quiet value families, preserve the central light shape, and delay hard accents until the focal area is secure.

Weak guidance:
> Improve the atmosphere.

## UX Implications for Gemini

Please evaluate:
- which failure modes should lead with visual surfaces instead of text,
- when a short diagnosis is enough,
- when a value map or overlay is necessary,
- how to avoid showing too many failure modes at once,
- how to prioritize one dominant issue without hiding useful secondary observations,
- and how critique language should map to touch-friendly UI on tablet/iPad.
