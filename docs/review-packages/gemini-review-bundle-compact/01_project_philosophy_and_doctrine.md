## Source Boundary: Painting Critique Philosophy

The purpose of critique is:
- to improve visual judgment,
- strengthen painterly decision-making,
- and accelerate artistic learning.

The goal is not:
- beautification,
- stylistic imitation,
- or automated art generation.

The system prioritizes:
1. shape design,
2. value structure,
3. edge economy,
4. atmospheric coherence,
5. color orchestration,
6. detail.

AI functions as:
- diagnostic assistant,
- correction simulator,
- visual reasoning partner,
- and experimentation tool.

AI is not:
- a replacement painter,
- a one-click artwork generator,
- or a decorative filter system.

The core loop is:
1. upload painting,
2. diagnose structural problems,
3. demonstrate selective corrections,
4. compare alternatives,
5. repaint manually.

The repaint is considered essential.

The critique philosophy favors:
- abstraction,
- simplification,
- atmosphere,
- and shape economy

over:
- literal copying,
- exhaustive detail,
- and photographic rendering.

Guiding question:
> Does this help the painter see and decide better?

## Source Boundary: Artist Influences

Artist influences are used pedagogically, structurally, and conceptually. The goal is principle extraction, not imitation.

### Edward Wesson

Wesson strongly influenced:
- atmospheric simplification,
- shape economy,
- connected washes,
- selective detail,
- and painterly editing.

Major lessons:
- complex scenes can often be reduced into a few dominant masses,
- shadow structures should connect,
- broad tonal groupings matter more than object-by-object description,
- not everything deserves description,
- edge economy supports focal hierarchy,
- quiet backgrounds and merged secondary forms strengthen atmosphere.

Palette tendency:
- muted earths,
- restrained blues,
- atmospheric greys,
- warm neutrals,
- limited saturation.

High chroma is used sparingly.

### Edward Seago

Seago influenced:
- atmosphere,
- tonal subtlety,
- restrained contrast,
- emotional quietness,
- and spatial depth.

Major lessons:
- high-key structure can still have clear value relationships,
- atmosphere often emerges from omission,
- chroma and values should remain controlled,
- restraint can be more powerful than description.

### Line-and-Wash Traditions

Line-and-wash traditions emphasize:
- selective drawing,
- rhythm of marks,
- structural economy,
- and wash dominance.

Key principle:
- lines should support washes, not imprison them.

### Restrained Palette Doctrine

Repeatedly preferred:
- limited pigments,
- neutralized mixtures,
- warm/cool orchestration,
- atmospheric greys.

Limited palettes improve harmony, reduce chromatic chaos, and simplify decision-making.

### Atmospheric Simplification

Core doctrine:
> Simplification creates atmosphere.

Methods include:
- reducing edge count,
- compressing detail,
- muting secondary chroma,
- merging distant forms,
- simplifying backgrounds.

These influences collectively shape the system toward:
- painterliness over realism,
- atmosphere over description,
- structure over detail,
- and abstraction over literal transcription.

## Source Boundary: Watercolor Doctrine

Watercolor is treated as a medium of:
- controlled surrender,
- simplification,
- transparency,
- and selective control.

The goal is not:
- total precision,
- excessive correction,
- or over-rendered realism.

### Freshness

Freshness is fragile, valuable, and easily destroyed.

Common threats:
- repeated correction,
- excessive lifting,
- scrubbing,
- patching muddy passages.

Recurring principle:
> Repainting fresh is often better than patching endlessly.

### White Preservation

Watercolor light usually comes from preserved paper, not added opacity later.

Preferred workflow:
- preserve important whites early,
- simplify around light shapes,
- avoid late-stage rescue attempts.

### Edge Economy

Edges are primary compositional tools.

Preferred:
- soft atmospheric transitions,
- selective hard edges,
- lost-and-found contours,
- varied edge behavior.

Avoid:
- outlining mentality,
- equal sharpness everywhere,
- excessive contour definition.

### Wash Unity

Good washes typically appear confident, economical, and unified.

Preferred handling:
- larger connected passages,
- fewer unnecessary interruptions,
- decisive brush movement.

Avoid:
- timid patchwork washes,
- repetitive touching,
- fragmented application.

### Glazing

Glazing is preferred over scrubbing, aggressive lifting, or muddy repainting.

Useful glazing goals:
- chroma hierarchy,
- atmospheric cooling,
- shadow unification,
- value deepening.

### Paper Integrity

Paper fatigue is serious. Once surface vitality is lost, repainting is often preferable.

### Watercolor Guiding Question

At every stage ask:
> Is this passage becoming fresher or more labored?

## Selected Glossary Terms for Workflow Review

### Atmospheric Simplification

Omission, softening, value compression, and reduced detail in service of air, depth, and visual calm. It does not mean making the painting vague everywhere.

### Chroma Hierarchy

Ordered control of saturation so color supports focus, depth, and atmosphere. High chroma should be limited and intentional.

### Dominant Mass

A large readable shape or value family that gives the painting structural authority.

### Edge Economy

Selective use of hard, soft, lost, and broken edges to control attention and atmosphere.

### Focal Hierarchy

Ordered control of contrast, edge sharpness, chroma, and detail density so the eye knows where to enter, pause, and move on.

### Painterliness

Visual authority through selection, abstraction, brush-led decision-making, and restraint rather than literal transcription.

### Repaint Workflow

The learning loop of diagnosis, simplified planning, manual repainting, and comparison.

### Repaintability

The practical clarity of what a painter should do next in a new attempt.

### Selective Intervention

A local, interpretable correction that teaches the painter what to change and why.

### Visual Noise

Unnecessary activity that competes with the painting's larger structure.

## UX Implications for Gemini

Please evaluate how the interface can:
- keep painterliness visible without turning it into vague looseness,
- make watercolor freshness and paper fatigue legible as UX constraints,
- prevent artist influence language from becoming style imitation,
- support restraint without making the workflow feel passive,
- and keep the repaint, not AI output, as the natural endpoint.
