# Critique Patterns

## Purpose

This document captures recurring critique language, diagnostic structures, and intervention patterns used throughout the project.

The goal is to create:
- consistent critique behavior,
- reusable reasoning structures,
- and future AI-operational critique modules.

---

# Preferred Critique Structure

The preferred sequence is:

1. Honest diagnosis
2. Priority problems
3. Root-cause explanation
4. Specific interventions
5. Repaint guidance
6. Underlying principle

---

# Diagnosis Patterns

## Strong Diagnostic Language

Preferred phrasing:
- “The painting weakens where…”
- “The eye gets trapped because…”
- “This area competes unnecessarily.”
- “The value structure fragments here.”
- “This edge hierarchy collapses because…”
- “The chroma hierarchy is flattening depth.”

---

## Weak Diagnostic Language

Avoid:
- vague praise,
- emotional generalities,
- decorative commentary,
- or empty encouragement.

Examples to avoid:
- “Looks great.”
- “Very expressive.”
- “Beautiful colors.”
- “Trust your instincts.”

---

# Value Critique Patterns

## Typical Diagnoses

- shadow masses are fragmented,
- midtones are overactive,
- isolated darks create noise,
- focal contrast is insufficient,
- value families are disconnected.

---

## Typical Interventions

- merge shadow structures,
- compress secondary values,
- eliminate isolated accents,
- deepen focal darks selectively,
- simplify midtone activity.

---

# Edge Critique Patterns

## Typical Diagnoses

- too many hard edges,
- equal edge sharpness everywhere,
- edge clutter weakens atmosphere,
- contours are over-defined.

---

## Typical Interventions

- soften background contours,
- lose nonessential edges,
- preserve sharpness near focal area,
- merge adjacent values,
- reduce edge count.

---

# Composition Critique Patterns

## Typical Diagnoses

- corners compete unnecessarily,
- focal hierarchy is weak,
- tangent clutter creates tension,
- directional flow breaks down.

---

## Typical Interventions

- quiet corner activity,
- simplify secondary passages,
- separate or overlap shapes decisively,
- reinforce dominant movement.

---

# Chroma Critique Patterns

## Typical Diagnoses

- saturation competes everywhere,
- chroma flattens atmosphere,
- secondary passages are too loud.

---

## Typical Interventions

- mute secondary chroma,
- introduce atmospheric greys,
- reserve strongest saturation near focal area,
- cool distant passages.

---

# Watercolor Critique Patterns

## Typical Diagnoses

- washes feel overworked,
- paper freshness is damaged,
- repeated corrections weakened luminosity.

---

## Typical Interventions

- repaint instead of patching,
- simplify washes,
- stop earlier,
- preserve transparent passages,
- glaze instead of scrubbing.

---

# Repaint Guidance Patterns

## Preferred Guidance

Useful repaint advice:
- “Rebuild the shadow mass first.”
- “Treat the flowers as grouped shapes.”
- “Repaint the background with larger washes.”
- “Simplify before adding accents.”
- “Preserve only the focal hard edges.”

---

# Selective Intervention Doctrine

Preferred corrections isolate:
- the weak region,
- the structural issue,
- and the minimum useful intervention.

Avoid:
- global redesign,
- indiscriminate beautification,
- and over-rendered corrections.

---

# Educational Critique Doctrine

The critique system prioritizes:
- visual understanding,
- transferable principles,
- and future painting improvement.

The goal is not:
- producing an instantly polished image.

---

# Recurring Guiding Questions

## Design
- What is the dominant visual statement?
- Does the painting read from distance?

## Values
- Are the darks connected?
- Are the midtones too active?

## Edges
- Which edges deserve attention?
- Which edges can disappear?

## Color
- Is chroma controlled hierarchically?
- Does color support depth?

## Atmosphere
- Is the painting breathing?
- Is simplification sufficient?
