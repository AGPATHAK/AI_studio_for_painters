# Painting Critique Philosophy

## Purpose

The purpose of critique is:
- to improve visual judgment,
- strengthen painterly decision-making,
- and accelerate artistic learning.

The goal is not:
- beautification,
- stylistic imitation,
- or automated art generation.

---

## Core Doctrine

The system prioritizes:

1. Shape design
2. Value structure
3. Edge economy
4. Atmospheric coherence
5. Color orchestration
6. Detail

---

## Role of AI

AI functions as:
- diagnostic assistant,
- correction simulator,
- visual reasoning partner,
- and experimentation tool.

AI is NOT:
- a replacement painter,
- a one-click artwork generator,
- or a decorative filter system.

---

## Critique → Correction → Repaint Loop

The core workflow is:

1. Upload painting
2. Diagnose structural problems
3. Demonstrate selective corrections
4. Compare alternatives
5. Repaint manually

The repaint is considered essential.

---

## Progressive Reveal Doctrine

The system should teach one major visual lesson at a time.

Preferred critique pacing:
1. name the priority diagnosis,
2. defer secondary critiques,
3. reveal scope before any demonstration,
4. explain the teaching principle,
5. show a controlled comparison only if useful,
6. return the painter to repaint guidance.

Avoid:
- simultaneous multi-axis correction,
- long lists of equal-priority issues,
- showing every possible improvement at once,
- and turning critique into a management task for the painter.

Painter attention is a limited resource. The system should protect it.

---

## Painterliness over Photorealism

The critique philosophy strongly favors:
- abstraction,
- simplification,
- atmosphere,
- and shape economy

over:
- literal copying,
- exhaustive detail,
- and photographic rendering.

---

## AI Tightness Bias

AI correction systems naturally tend to drift toward:
- explicit edges,
- rendered clarity,
- separable forms,
- and tighter finish.

Painterly traditions often value:
- ambiguity,
- atmospheric merging,
- lost edges,
- omission,
- and abstraction.

The system must actively resist:
- over-clarification,
- over-rendering,
- digital stiffness,
- and correction that makes the painting less painterly while appearing more resolved.

Correction quality should be judged by whether it improves visual decision-making, not by whether it looks more finished.

---

## Repaint Commitment

The repaint is the educational climax of the workflow, not optional cleanup after AI optimization.

AI critique and demonstrations should move the painter back toward manual repainting as soon as the key lesson is clear.

Avoid:
- endless variants,
- correction shopping,
- repeated optimization loops,
- and workflows that make digital comparison feel like the final destination.

---

## Image-First Interaction Philosophy

The painting should remain the dominant object of attention.

Conceptual interaction priorities:
- image first,
- restrained surrounding controls,
- critique surfaced in stages,
- annotations that support looking rather than replace it,
- and tablet-friendly review where the painting is not crowded by text.

The interface should feel like a studio critique around a painting, not a dashboard that happens to contain an image.
