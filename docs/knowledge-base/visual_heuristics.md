# Visual Heuristics

## Purpose

This document captures the recurring visual reasoning principles used throughout the critique system.

These heuristics are intended to:
- improve painterly judgment,
- support critique consistency,
- and guide AI-assisted correction workflows.

---

# Value Heuristics

## Read from Distance First

A painting should remain readable:
- at thumbnail size,
- from across the room,
- and under squinting.

Loss of readability usually indicates:
- fragmentation,
- weak mass grouping,
- or excessive detail competition.

---

## Preserve Dominant Masses

Strong paintings usually contain:
- a few dominant connected shapes,
- not many isolated fragments.

Preferred:
- connected shadow families,
- unified midtone structures,
- simplified light groupings.

Avoid:
- salt-and-pepper value noise,
- disconnected dark accents,
- excessive tonal interruptions.

---

## Merge Shadows Aggressively

Shadows should usually behave as:
- large architectural structures,
not:
- individual object descriptions.

Recurring doctrine:
> Merge first, separate later only if necessary.

---

## Compress Secondary Values

Not all areas deserve full tonal range.

Reduce value contrast in:
- background passages,
- secondary objects,
- corner regions,
- and distant forms.

Reserve strongest contrast for focal hierarchy.

---

# Edge Heuristics

## Hard Edges Are Expensive

Hard edges attract attention.

Use them selectively:
- near focal areas,
- major plane changes,
- or critical structural accents.

Avoid equal edge sharpness everywhere.

---

## Lose More Edges Than Feels Comfortable

Atmosphere often improves when:
- contours dissolve,
- adjacent values merge,
- and forms partially disappear.

Especially useful in:
- backgrounds,
- distant foliage,
- sky transitions,
- and shadow passages.

---

## Edge Variety Creates Life

Good paintings contain:
- lost edges,
- soft edges,
- broken edges,
- and selective sharp accents.

Uniform edge treatment creates stiffness.

---

# Composition Heuristics

## Protect Focal Hierarchy

The eye should know:
- where to enter,
- where to pause,
- and where not to linger.

Focal hierarchy is controlled through:
- value contrast,
- edge sharpness,
- chroma,
- and detail density.

---

## Quiet the Corners

Corners should rarely compete with the focal area.

Reduce:
- contrast,
- edge activity,
- texture,
- and chroma near edges.

---

## Avoid Tangent Clutter

Touching shapes create visual tension.

Preferred:
- clear overlap,
- clear separation,
- or deliberate merging.

Avoid accidental alignments.

---

# Chroma Heuristics

## Chroma Must Be Budgeted

High saturation everywhere weakens atmosphere.

Reserve strongest chroma for:
- focal regions,
- emotional accents,
- or deliberate compositional emphasis.

---

## Neutralize Secondary Passages

Backgrounds and supporting areas usually benefit from:
- muted mixtures,
- atmospheric greys,
- and lower saturation.

---

## Temperature Supports Depth

Typical atmospheric pattern:
- warmer foregrounds,
- cooler distance,
- softer distant transitions.

---

# Simplification Heuristics

## Paint Masses Before Objects

Large shape relationships matter more than:
- object identity,
- decorative marks,
- or local detail.

---

## Remove Before Adding

Recurring critique question:
> What can be removed without harming the painting?

Simplification usually strengthens:
- atmosphere,
- clarity,
- and visual authority.

---

# Watercolor Heuristics

## Freshness Is Fragile

Repeated correction often damages:
- luminosity,
- wash clarity,
- and surface vitality.

When overworked:
- repainting is often better than patching.

---

## Preserve Whites Early

Watercolor light is usually planned, not recovered.

Protect paper whites before complexity develops.

---

# AI-Assisted Critique Heuristics

## Corrections Should Be Selective

Avoid global “improvement.”

Preferred:
- local interventions,
- interpretable changes,
- and controlled demonstrations.

---

## Demonstrate, Do Not Replace

AI corrections exist to:
- teach,
- clarify,
- and guide repaint decisions.

They are not the final artwork.
